# Introduction

The global telecommunications industry is undergoing significant structural transformation. Traditional revenue sources such as voice and SMS continue to decline, while data consumption grows rapidly alongside the acceleration of societal digitalization. At the same time, operators are required to continuously invest in new technologies — 5G, fixed-mobile convergence (FMC), and digital ecosystems — while maintaining operational efficiency and their sustainability commitments.

Amid this dynamic, developments in digital technology, automation, and artificial intelligence particularly Agentic AI are opening new transformation opportunities. These technologies enable operators to improve operational efficiency, strengthen customer experience, and create new business models that are more adaptive to market changes.

<!-- PDF page 6 -->

# Company Overview

Telkomsel is Indonesia's largest cellular and fixed broadband service provider and a leading player in driving national digital transformation. As a subsidiary of PT Telkom Indonesia Tbk (69.9%) and Singtel Mobile (30.1%), Telkomsel reinforces its role in delivering inclusive and sustainable digital connectivity. Founded in 1995, Telkomsel serves more than 159 million cellular subscribers and approximately 8.5 million fixed broadband subscribers.

On the infrastructure side, Telkomsel is supported by a nationwide network of more than 271,000 BTS (including 221,000+ 4G/LTE BTS) with coverage exceeding 97% of the population. 5G rollout continues to accelerate (projected at approximately 5,000–5,300 5G BTS by 2025). Total data payload has exceeded 20 million TB per year.

# Case Background

Although still the market leader, Telkomsel faces increasingly complex business pressures amid shifting industry dynamics, technological developments, and rising customer expectations. The challenge extends beyond growth to include maintaining profitability, improving operational efficiency, and fulfilling long-term sustainability commitments. Key challenges include:

- **5G Monetization Challenge**  
  The substantial 5G investment (spectrum, infrastructure modernization, edge computing) puts pressure on ROI and the sustainability of the 5G business model.
- **Competitive Pressure & Profitability**  
  The market is highly price-sensitive, with competition centered on price pressure. Being the market leader alone is no longer sufficient; new, sustainable sources of growth are required.
- **Infrastructure Operational Complexity**  
  Significant energy consumption, network maintenance costs, BTS operations in remote/off-grid areas, and a growing carbon footprint.
- **Changing Customer Expectations**  
  Customers expect fast, seamless digital experiences, real-time personalization, and proactive problem resolution, while many legacy processes remain siloed, reactive, fragmented, and bureaucratic

Developments in digital technology, particularly Agentic AI, present an opportunity for Telkomsel to transform its operations while creating new business value.

# Challenge Context

## MyTelkomsel and Veronika as Digital Channels

MyTelkomsel is Telkomsel's super app, covering package and add-on purchases, payments (QRIS and e-wallet), Halo and IndiHome billing, the Telkomsel Poin loyalty program, entertainment (video, music, games), and lifestyle features. The application has been downloaded more than 100 million times, with approximately 50 million monthly active users, making it the primary digital touchpoint for customers.

Veronika is an AI-based virtual assistant that has been in operation since 2017 and, since 2023, has been integrated with Microsoft Azure OpenAI Service. Veronika handles product inquiries, troubleshooting, and package recommendations through MyTelkomsel, WhatsApp, and Instagram, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Since the AI enhancement, the proportion of interactions resolved through self-service has increased and call center load has decreased.

## GraPARI: Value and Cost of Physical Service Points

GraPARI is Telkomsel's network of physical service outlets. Customers visit in person for needs such as SIM card replacement, service upgrades, claims, complex complaint handling, data updates, and other administrative matters. This physical presence entails outlet operating costs (rent, electricity, cooling, staffing) and — an often-overlooked factor — emissions and time associated with customer travel to the outlet, queuing, and paper usage (forms, receipts, transaction slips).

<!-- PDF page 10 -->

Some of these needs can technically already be resolved through digital channels, but self-service adoption remains uneven: some customers still choose, or feel they need, to visit GraPARI in person due to limited digital literacy, lack of trust in digital processes, or because their case type is not yet supported by self-service.

## Why This Is Also a Green Economy Issue

Telkomsel targets a 20% reduction in carbon emissions intensity by 2030, alongside expanded digital access to support national inclusion. Shifting service interactions from physical visits to digital/AI channels has the potential to reduce indirect emissions (including those from customer travel), lower outlet energy consumption, and eliminate paper use.

## Business Perspective

- **Monetization Gap**  
  Data traffic is growing +18% to +25% per year, while blended ARPU has moved only from Rp45,000 to Rp49,000. Value-added digital services are needed, beyond connectivity alone.
- **Competitive Pressure**  
  Market share is declining (46% → 44%) in a price-sensitive market; non-price differentiation is becoming critical.
- **Customer Expectations**  
  Fast, seamless, personal, and proactive service are difficult to meet with a service model still reliant on physical outlets and reactive processes.

# Problem Statement

How can Telkomsel develop AI-based, consumer-facing self-service digital services (built on the foundation of MyTelkomsel and Veronika) that create real value for individual customers and unlock new sources of business growth, while measurably reducing customers' need to visit GraPARI in person and lowering the carbon footprint of service delivery, as a contribution to the transition toward a green economy?

## Guiding Questions

*(Participants Must Address These, But Are Not Limited to Them)*

1. Which types of services or use cases should be prioritized for migration from GraPARI to digital/AI channels, and which should remain physically served, and why?
2. How should the MyTelkomsel and Veronika experience be designed so that non-digital-native customers are both willing and able to use self-service (digital inclusion)?
3. What new digital services could be monetized (e.g., subscriptions, value-added services, personalization), and what would the corresponding revenue model look like?
4. What methodology should be used to measure environmental impact (CO₂e emissions avoided through reduced physical visits, outlet energy, and paper use) and how should this be integrated into the company's ESG reporting? Can this impact serve as a selling point (green branding, customer engagement)?
5. How should the role and format of GraPARI evolve (e.g., shifting from routine transactions toward experience, retention, education, or MSME empowerment)?
6. What are the key trade-offs and risks (decline in service quality for complex cases, data security and privacy, fraud, customer/employee resistance), and how should they be mitigated?

# Scope & Limitations

- Focus on the B2C segment (prepaid and postpaid).
- Solutions must leverage existing assets (MyTelkomsel, Veronika, Azure OpenAI, Telkomsel Poin, digital ecosystem) not build an entirely new channel from scratch.
- Solutions must balance five dimensions: customer value, business impact, technical feasibility, execution readiness, and environmental impact.
- Environmental impact claims must be accompanied by explainable assumptions, emission factors, and sources (following a GHG Protocol-type approach).

| Environmental Impact | Estimated tons of CO₂e avoided per year; reduction in paper consumption and outlet energy use; contribution to the -20% intensity target by 2030 |
